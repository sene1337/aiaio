DESIGN NOTES

What changes vs the side-scroller

  • 2D horizontal terrain → 2.5D isometric palace: one room per conversation chapter / topic.
  • Floors are the chapter's actual text as dim monospace tiles; furniture = tool calls; desks = real user asks; doorways = topic transitions.
  • The wall no longer eats a terrain column — it eats whole rooms. You can look back through a doorway and watch a room you just solved dissolve.
  • Play is room-to-room navigation + spatial routing, not pure run-and-gun.

What stays (invariant)

  • SessionCard → deterministic level; wall of forgetting = context pressure made spatial.
  • Token economy ctx meter; token spend advances the room-rot.
  • ⚡ compaction surge + amnesia — now a wing-collapse set-piece.
  • Task queue = real user asks with ▰▱ progress; ✳ subagents cost upkeep; error-log bestiary; TUI / Claude-Code look + WebAudio.

Why it could be MORE fun

  • Rooms = chapters you recognize — the palace is legible, so loss is legible.
  • Genuine dread: watch a room you just cleared garble and drop off the map.
  • Routing puzzle: send ✳ subagents into a doomed room to grab crates before it's forgotten.
  • Wing-collapse compaction is a real gut-punch, not a slider.

Top 2 risks

  • Engineering / perf: iso render + per-tile corruption + text-tile floors is a big lift vs the side-scroller; tiny tiles may be unreadable at speed.
  • Pacing: room-based traversal risks feeling like a walking-sim — enemies and wall pressure must stay high or the arcade urgency drains.
