DESIGN NOTES

What changes vs the current side-scroller

  • Axis flips to vertical. The timeline stops being a floor you walk and becomes a scrollback you fall through — the terrain is the rendered transcript, line by line.
  • The wall comes from ABOVE, and that's the point. Real context windows evict the oldest tokens first — the head of the buffer. So the corruption chewing downward from the top isn't arbitrary, it's literally how forgetting works. You rappel toward process exit 0 at the bottom while your session's opening dissolves overhead. (Below-rising was the alt; top-eviction won for accuracy and the ceiling-collapse dread.)
  • Platforms have grammar. Your asks are thick green ledges you can stand on; assistant text is thin dim footing; tool calls are bracketed scaffolding you clamber. Reading the session becomes literal traversal.

What stays (invariants, untouched)

  • Task-queue race with ▰▱ progress · the wall of forgetting (red static + tendrils) · token economy (ctx meter, every action bills) · ⚡ compaction surges · ✳ subagents · the little walking-terminal avatar (v2 title-bar, >_ face, antenna) · full Claude-Code TUI aesthetic.

Why it could be MORE fun

  • Downwell/rappel flow gives constant momentum — descent is the default verb, so idle = death in a way "stop walking" never quite sold.
  • Vertical legibility. You can see your whole recent history stacked above you and the forgetting front eating specific remembered lines — the stakes are the actual words, right there.
  • Perch-to-work makes stations feel like rope-stops: you brake your fall, root heads-down (weakest), ship the task, kick off again.

Top 2 risks

  • Readability vs. speed. Fast vertical scroll + tiny transcript type can turn into unreadable smear. Needs a comfortable fall cadence, snap-to-ledge, and a "quiet zone" band around the avatar where text stays crisp.
  • Camera/orientation. Danger overhead + gravity down is unusual; players may fight the camera. Mitigate with a persistent top-edge threat gauge and generous coyote-time on ledges.
