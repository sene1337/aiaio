DESIGN NOTES — the pitch, in the open

WHAT CHANGES vs the side-scroller

  • The timeline stops being a left→right corridor and becomes a closed loop — you lap it, you never reach a far wall, you manage a chase.
  • The Wall of Forgetting is no longer a vertical curtain behind you — it's a radar sweep-hand pinned to the center, rotating only when you burn tokens. Spatially it reads as a clock you're racing.
  • /compact and involuntary compaction physically shrink the ring — the whole world contracts, contents re-pack tighter, near-misses become collisions. A ◈ model upgrade grows it back. The context budget is now literally the size of your playfield.
  • Positioning is angular, not linear: you can retreat by lapping ahead into stale terrain the hand already ate — risky, but it buys distance.

WHY IT COULD BE MORE FUN

  • Legible pressure. One glance at the clock tells you exactly how much runway is left and where the hand will bite next — dread you can read, not guess.
  • The shrink is a genuine "oh no" beat. Watching your world contract mid-fight — stations sliding into your lap, the hand suddenly closer — is a compaction you feel, and it makes the ◈ upgrade euphoric.
  • Lap tactics. Do I finish this station or sprint a quarter-turn to dodge the sweep? Every second is a tempo call — pure arcade risk/reward that the corridor never quite forced.
  • Center-screen goal + ctx meter means the whole fantasy is always on camera — Tempest/Missile-Command readability, attract-mode ready.

TOP 2 RISKS

  • Readability at small ring sizes. After a compaction the track is tight and re-packed — station labels, enemies, and the avatar can overlap into mush. Needs aggressive label de-clutter (fan-out leaders, fade distant text) or the shrink turns fun into noise.
  • Orientation / motion sickness. A radial world where the camera may want to rotate with the player is disorienting; keeping the ring world-locked (as here) is safer but makes "behind you" less visceral than a side-scroller's over-the-shoulder wall.
