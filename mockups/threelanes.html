THE THREE LANES

The session plays forward and you live inside its flow — hop lanes to catch the work, dodge the log, and outrun the wall of forgetting eating the left edge. attract mode · autoplay
DESIGN NOTES

WHAT CHANGES vs the current side-scroller

  • The level is now a conveyor, not a place you roam — the transcript auto-scrolls right→left and you hold a near-fixed x.
  • Your only inputs collapse to ↑/↓ hop lane + W hold to go heads-down. No aiming, no terrain-drilling.
  • The wall stops being a chasing entity — it is the left screen edge. Falling behind the scroll = the edge eats you.
  • Token spend maps to belt speed, not just distance: burn tokens and the whole tape lurches faster.

WHAT STAYS (invariants)

  • Task queue race with real user asks + ▰▱ progress.
  • Wall of forgetting: red corruption + flickering static, tasks it passes are forgotten.
  • Token economy — ctx meter; spending accelerates.
  • ⚡ compaction = tape-skip glitch that tears unfinished windows.
  • Subagents ✳ hold a lane for you. Terminal-window avatar (traffic-lights, v2, >_ face, antenna). Moments & enemies mined from the real log.

WHY IT COULD BE MORE FUN

  • Instant-read dread: the failure state is right there on the left — no camera-hunting to know how doomed you are.
  • Greed vs sight: heads-down fills the bar fastest but blinds you to the next lane of hazards — a real choice every second.
  • One-thumb, pick-up-and-play; Canabalt/rhythm-runner tempo that reads great in attract mode and over-the-shoulder.
  • Compaction becomes a screen-wide beat drop you learn to brace for, not just a meter event.

TOP 2 RISKS

  • Depth loss. Three lanes may be too thin to carry AIAIO's richer systems (weapons, upgrade gambles, destructible terrain) — risk of flattening a sim into a plain runner.
  • Heads-down fights the fiction. "Locked in place while the tape scrolls" only feels fair if the work-window docks to you cleanly; get it wrong and going heads-down reads as punishment, not a gamble.
