THE RAIL MAP

your session log as a living metro network — route the signal-train to exit 0 before the forgetting swallows the line. ● attract mode
AIAIO · concept: rail-map · mini-metro meets terminal · ~20s loop
DESIGN NOTES — what this reimagines, what it keeps, why it's more fun

WHAT CHANGES vs the current side-scroller

  • Camera flips from a horizontal platformer to a top-down transit graph. You don't run — you route: movement is choosing which switch/edge to take at each junction.
  • The timeline becomes metro lines. Message turns are stations; threads of work are colored lines; the session's shape is legible at a glance.
  • Tool calls become literal side-tracks that loop back — you can dip onto them for a payoff and re-merge, at a token cost.
  • The wall of forgetting is no longer a vertical curtain behind you — it's a 2D corruption front sweeping the whole map from the origin station, garbling station names as it eats them.

WHAT STAYS (every invariant, intact)

  • Task-queue race — your real asks are interchange hubs with ▰▱ work progress you must stop and grind.
  • Token economy — a live ctx meter; every switch thrown and metre travelled spends tokens, and tokens are wall distance.
  • ⚡ Compaction events — glitch + the front surges forward; the ctx meter drains.
  • Subagents — ✳ helper trains on parallel branch lines that can corrupt and merge back badly.
  • The avatar — the terminal-window signal riding a rail car: title bar, traffic-light dots, v2 tag, blinking >_ face, antenna.
  • Bestiary from your error log — 👻 hallucination-ghost phases between lines, ⏱ timeout-blob stalls a track, ☢ regression-splitter lurks.
  • TUI / Claude-Code aesthetic — monospace, boxed panels, ⎿ transcript grammar, the exact palette.

WHY IT COULD BE MORE FUN

  • Decisions, not just dodging. Branch vs. main-line, dip the tool-loop or skip it, babysit a subagent or terminate it — every junction is a real choice under the ticking front.
  • The map IS the story, and it's different every session. A clean run is a tidy line; a rough one is a tangle of red branches and dead hubs — instant replay value and shareable topology.

TOP 2 RISKS

  • Readability / clutter. Metro map + garbling corruption + enemies + labels can collapse into noise — especially once the front starts scrambling names. Needs ruthless label culling and z-order discipline.
  • Loss of twitch tension. A routing game risks feeling turn-based and calm. The real-time pressure (front speed, timeout-blobs stalling live track, ghosts phasing your branch) has to stay genuinely stressful or it's a puzzle, not a game.
