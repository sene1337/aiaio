▸ DESIGN NOTES — the call-stack cut

What changes vs the current side-scroller

  • Axis flips to vertical. The timeline stops being ground you run across and becomes a tower you climb — the actual call stack. Frames push up as you go deeper, pop as calls return.
  • The wall becomes a tide. Forgetting stops chasing horizontally and rises from below as a lake of corruption. Same rule — every token burned raises it — but now it reads as drowning / sinking, not being outrun.
  • Compaction is a pancake-collapse. Instead of a shove forward, several lower frames crush together and the whole tower lurches down toward the lake. Voluntary /compact = a controlled drop; involuntary = a violent one.
  • Verticality is the difficulty. Falling is a real failure state (Icy-Tower / Doodle-Jump). Miss a jump between narrow scaffold frames and you fall into what you already forgot.

What stays (invariants, untouched)

  • Task queue race with real user asks + ▰▱ progress; the wall of forgetting as red static with reaching tendrils.
  • Token economy — the ctx meter, actions raise the tide; ⚡ compaction glitch; ✳ subagents fluttering; the >_ v2 terminal-window avatar; full TUI / Claude-Code look.
  • Errors as enemies on the frames they happened on (👻 ⏱ ☢); moments etched where they occurred.

Why it could be MORE fun

  • Height is legible dread. "How high can I climb before it drowns me" is instantly gripping and endlessly leaderboard-able — the side-scroller's win line is flat, a tower is a personal-best.
  • Return-pops are free comedy + rhythm. Watching a tool frame you climbed off collapse the instant it returns makes the stack feel alive and impatient, and rewards fast play.
  • Gravity makes every token hurt viscerally. The tide you can see gaining on your feet during a heads-down work-station is more honest terror than a wall to your left.
  • Subagent balconies add spatial risk/reward — step out onto a precarious side-frame for loot, but it's the first thing the tide eats.

Top 2 risks

  • Readability of the stack. A tall column of frames + a HUD + rising static can get noisy fast. Needs ruthless frame-labeling and a calm camera or it turns to mush.
  • Vertical platforming difficulty. Jump-between-frames adds a skill gate the horizontal version didn't have — if it feels like fighting the controls instead of the wall, it alienates the "watch my session become a level" crowd. Must stay forgiving.
